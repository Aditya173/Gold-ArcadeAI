# Idea 6 research: clubs, teams and student orgs -> a season drop from shared photos

Web research only: no photos read, no models run.

## 0. Verdict up front

**Keep it, narrowed.** As worded ("a group merch drop") it fails on product fit: clubs buy apparel
at $18–45 a piece, and Arcade makes no apparel. It passes as a **season keepsake**:

- **Group piece**, paid by the organizer from dues or a fundraiser: a rug, wall print or textile
  banner for the clubhouse or gym wall, from the season's palette and recurring scenes.
- **Personal piece**, paid by each member who opts in: a pendant or small print from the events
  *that member was at*.

A per-member price near Arcade's ~$100 floor only works where people already pay that for a season
memento: championship rings ($189–350), finisher posters ($37–217). It does not work as everyday
club merch.

**Weakest assumption:** that members of a social club pay about $100 for a piece that is not
clothing. The only evidence is adjacent (rings for teams that won, posters for runners who
finished). A club with no "moment" (race, season end, championship) has no evidence behind it.

**Biggest risk:** faces. Finding "which photos each member is in" by face is biometric processing of
people other than the phone's owner. The design must be **opt-in per member, on the member's own
action**, and never a scan of everyone in the album (§6).

### 2.3 Tools clubs use today

- **Per-member order forms:** Custom Ink Group Order Form ([Custom Ink](https://www.customink.com/help_center/collecting-sizes-and-payment-with-gof)).
- **Team stores with a fundraising markup:** SquadLocker adds 5–15% for the club ([SquadLocker](https://www.squadlocker.com/squad-blog/fundraising-through-squadlocker)); TeamSnap resells it ([TeamSnap](https://www.teamsnap.com/blog/teamsnap-features/the-power-of-youth-sports-fundraising)).
- **Pre-order windows:** Bonfire's 21-day campaigns print only what sold ([Bonfire](https://www.bonfire.com/how-it-works/)); Boring Run Club ran limited pre-orders ("I didn't want to buy 200 hoodies and sell 20", [Tapstitch](https://www.tapstitch.com/blog/post/boring-run-club)).
- **Creator storefronts:** Fourthwall, or Shopify plus Printful/Printify ([ecommerceceo](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/fourthwall-vs-printify/)).
- **Drops as events:** Endorphins Running's numbered drops ([Endorphins](https://shop.endorphinsrunning.com/products/drop-004-winter-running-beanie)); Tims Run Club sold out in minutes ([Tim Hortons](https://news.timhortons.ca/en/articles/tim-hortons-to-celebrate-national-coffee-day-on-sept-29-with-exclusive-limited-edition-tims-run-club-apparel)).

The drop mechanics (pre-order window, per-member payment, organizer markup) are solved. Copy the
pre-order window; made-to-order pieces never become inventory.

| Heylo | Group album; event cover photos | [Heylo](https://heylo.com/help/event-photos) |

**The input is the organizer's shared album, not anyone's whole camera roll.** A member's personal
piece reads only the album events that member says they were at.

**Copyright:** whoever took a photo generally owns it ([US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/photographers/)).
A shared album mixes many photographers. Pieces *inspired by* the photos (palette, scenes) don't
reproduce them; a print that uses a member's photo directly needs that photographer's OK.

## 4. Which Arcade categories fit

| Piece | Category | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Group: clubhouse or gym-wall piece | Rug, Wall Art Print, Textile (banner) | Best fit; one per club, organizer pays |
| Group: raffle prize | Rug, Throw Pillow | Plausible; no evidence on how clubs run raffles |
| Personal: season pendant | Pendant Necklace | Closest to the championship ring. Jewelry takes no `colors`, so the motif lives in the prompt |
| Personal: "your season" print | Wall Art Print | Closest to the finisher poster |
| Pins, apparel | none | Not in Arcade's catalogue as far as public sources show |

Press says Arcade "actively blocks trademarked names and logos" ([The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/news/ai-made-that-rug-and-those-earrings));
whether a small club's own logo is blocked is unconfirmed, and university groups face licensing rules
([Harvard trademark guidelines](https://trademark.harvard.edu/guidelines-ordering-internal-insignia-items-swag)).
Design around it: palette and recurring scenes, not the logo.

## 5. What the Gold SDK adds, honestly

- **Finding "my photos" in an album is already solved** (Google Photos face groups, Waldo, race-photo
  vendors). It is not a differentiator, and this idea does not do it.
- **What a reading can add:** the season's signature and palette from the whole album (what sets
  this club's photos apart), and a member's own slice from the events they tagged. Whether a club
  album has a signature distinct from "people outdoors in sportswear", and whether two members'
  slices differ enough to give different pieces, is **untested** without a real club album.

## 6. Privacy and consent (the deciding constraint)

- **Face templates of people other than the user are biometric data.** Illinois BIPA carries
  $1,000/$5,000 statutory damages per violation; Google paid $100M over Photos face grouping
  ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/google-to-pay-settlement-to-illinois-residents-for-bipa-violations));
  the FTC made Everalbum delete its face models ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2021/05/ftc-finalizes-settlement-photo-app-developer-related-misuse-facial-recognition-technology)).
  GDPR Article 9 treats biometric data as a special category.
- **The model that works is opt-in per person** (Waldo: each person or parent opts in themselves;
  others can stay out; [GearBrain](https://www.gearbrain.com/amp/waldo-camp-facial-recognition-software-2589892014),
  [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5245616)). Privacy advocates still object.
- **So:** the group piece uses no people at all. A member's piece comes from events the member tags
  themself into, under an anonymous ref, after they opt in. Nobody is matched by face. Minors are
  out of scope.
