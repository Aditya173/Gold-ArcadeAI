# Idea 6: open questions

The honest blockers from the reviews.

1. **Clubs buy $18–45 apparel; there is no evidence for $100+ wall pieces or pendants.** Arcade makes
   no apparel or pins. The only evidence for $100+ is adjacent: championship rings and finisher
   posters, both tied to a moment. A club with no race, season end or anniversary has nothing behind
   it.
2. **No real club album has been read.** Everything so far is mechanics on an invented club. Whether
   a real album has a signature that sets it apart from any group of people outdoors in sportswear is
   the gate before more building.
3. **Do the photos add more than a palette?** Ask the organizer "What does your club do together?"
   before they see the drop. If their answer already names every theme, the photos add only the
   colours.
4. **Do members' seasons differ enough?** Members of one club share most events, so their slices may
   look alike. The "stands out" rule (1.5x the club's share) is a guess; it needs a real test against
   random same-size sets of the club's events.
5. **Self-tagging is honest but incomplete.** Members may tick too few events, or the wrong ones.
   Opt-in "find me" by one selfie would help, but only with explicit consent, only for that member,
   and it is not built.
6. **No buyer asked yet.** No organizer has priced a group piece and no member has said yes to a
   pendant at about $100.
7. **The rug has no real price.** A printed rug's Arcade price is unknown; don't offer it to an
   organizer before a quote exists. The wall print is the piece with a believable floor.
8. **Photographers' consent** in a shared album is collected by the organizer; the flow for asking
   them (and the people in frame) is not built.
9. **Logo rules** for a small club's own mark, and licensing for university groups, are unconfirmed.
