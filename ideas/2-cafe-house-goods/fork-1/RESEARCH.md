# Idea 2, fork 1 research: the diner, not the café

Market work for cafés is in `../RESEARCH.md`. This covers what changes when the buyer is the person who
eats out.

## What people buy today

Custom tea towels are cheap:
- Printify personalized tea towels "From $17.13" ([Printify](https://printify.com/personalized-kitchen-towels)).
- Qstomize custom photo tea towel "$17.95 USD" ([Qstomize](https://www.qstomize.com/products/custom-photo-tea-towel)).
- Etsy recipe towels: "$16 per recipe" and "$22.00+" ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/217433682), [Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/736115942)).

Custom food art is a freelance trade: a custom ramen illustration with source files at "$60"
([Gumroad](https://angleyouraim.gumroad.com/l/ramen-soup)).

**What this means:**
- **Weakest assumption:** a mock $55 textile costs 2.5–6× a custom towel. The line only works if "made by
  an artisan from your own table life" is worth that premium. Unmeasured.
- The print and the plate are likeliest to clear the price bar; they already sell as art. The towel is the
  gift-sized entry point but competes with $15 towels.
- No competitor reads the person's photos. Recipe towels use a typed recipe; photo towels print one photo.
  The gap is choosing the motif from many photos and showing why.

## Consent

- The diner consents for their own roll; their companions do not, so companions never appear in a piece
  or any shared evidence.
- The gift page names no places, shows no photos and gives no counts: a gifter learns nothing about where
  the person eats.
