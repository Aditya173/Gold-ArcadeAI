# Idea 2 research: independent cafés and restaurants → a house goods line

Public market research only. "UNSOURCED" means no public evidence was found, not that the claim is
false. Nothing here has been tested with an owner or a guest.

## Verdict: keep, with a reshaped buyer

**"A café sells a house line to its guests" is the weak version.** Café merch sells at $15–$46, while
Arcade's listed print and textile prices start around $69 (a pillow cover) and reach $576 (a wool rug).
**The strong version is the owner buying for their own room first**: an entrance rug, a wall print, a
banquette pillow, a decorative plate, priced against a branding agency ($2k–$25k) or a commissioned
potter (3–6× the cost of white china). A small guest shelf comes second, limited to pieces already made
for the room: the "buy the plate you ate off" model that Heath × Tartine and The Progress have shown.
The diner (a person whose roll is full of meals) is the alternative buyer: `fork-1/`.

| Question | Answer | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | The owner, for the room (B2B). Guests buy a few pieces at retail | Owner: plausible. Guests at Arcade prices: **weakest assumption** |
| Why photos and not a form | Photos of the room show its materials and palette; a menu form can't | Hypothesis: no café has been read |
| Which photos | A deliberate owner shoot of one room (15–30 photos), or a roll where one food place clearly dominates | Clear |
| Which categories | Rug, Wall Art Print, Throw Pillow; Ceramic Plate and Textile *if they exist* | Ceramic and Textile are unconfirmed |

## 1. Market and who pays

**Size.** Technomic counted **412,498 US independent restaurant locations** at the end of 2025, down
2.3% that year ([Technomic, p. 3](https://pages.winsightmedia.com/rs/561-ZNP-897/images/US-Industry-Insights-Technomic-020926.pdf)).
A directory lists about **65,000 independent US coffee shops** ([Joe](https://joe.coffee/locations/),
a directory count). The Census counted **12,487 mobile-food employer establishments** in 2023
([Census](https://data.census.gov/profile/72233_-_Mobile_food_services?n=72233)).

**How many already sell merch** (payment-software vendors describing their own customers; no method stated):
- Square (Q3 2025): "22.9%" of cafés offer merch, against 56.5% of breweries and 17% of bars; t-shirts
  58.2% of the item mix ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/fall-restaurant-report-2025)).
- Lightspeed (2026): "nearly 20%" of hospitality businesses sell branded merch; for some it is "as much
  as 11% of monthly revenue" ([Retail Insider](https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2026/03/restaurant-merch-becoming-increasingly-more-popular-lightspeed-commerce/)).
- One case: merchandise was 30% of revenue at a café that doubles as a gallery (2016) ([Fresh Cup](https://freshcup.com/merchandising/)).

**What guests say they want.** 20% of diners want home goods "like candles and glassware", 19% branded
apparel ([SevenRooms](https://sevenrooms.com/blog/restaurant-merchandise); sample size not stated).

**Retail price norms** (asking prices, not measured spend):
- Russ & Daughters storefront mug $20 ([Russ & Daughters](https://russdaughters.toast.site/order/russanddaughters/item-passover-desserts_72422e5e-8de8-40fc-8e30-869c90a99eef));
  Levain tote $15, tee $35, candle $46 ([Levain](https://levainbakery.com/collections/merch)).
- Philadelphia: Mish Mish tote $38, candle $28; Herman's Coffee ashtray $65 ([Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/food/a/philly-restaurant-merch-trend-middle-child-kismet-20240814.html)).
- Ceramics: Heath dessert bowl in Tartine Teal $45 ([Heath](https://www.heathceramics.com/products/dessert-bowl-2?variant=6967132389409));
  The Progress dinner plate $46, salad plate $38 ([Remodelista](https://www.remodelista.com/posts/ceramics-from-the-progress/)).

**What the owner pays for the room today:**
- Brand identity: an independent designer $2,000–$8,000, a boutique agency $5,000–$25,000
  (agency-published ranges) ([iDesign](https://idesignny.com/blog/branding-cost-small-business), [Tenet](https://www.wearetenet.com/restaurant-branding-agency)).
- Commissioned plates: handmade pottery costs "three to six times" standard white china
  ([Edible Brooklyn](https://ediblebrooklyn.com/?p=75569)); commissions run to hundreds of pieces over
  months ([Cincinnati Magazine](https://www.cincinnatimagazine.com/article/meet-four-local-potters-who-set-the-table-at-cincinnati-restaurants/)).
- Entrance logo mats $41–$230 ([Webstaurant](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/69799/custom-floor-mats.html)).
  A tufted logo rug is roughly $20–$65 per sq ft by one maker's informal estimate ([Signs101](https://www.signs101.com/threads/custom-rugs-is-there-a-market.91355)).
- Logo napkins: minimums of 50–250 units at AU$6–$14 each ([Sense2](https://sense2.com/collections/custom-napkins)).

**Arcade's listed prices against these:** pillow cover $69, silk scarf $88, silver ring $178, ceramic
art $174, 14k necklace $490, wool rug $576 ([arcade.ai listings](https://www.arcade.ai/)); a seller keeps
any markup plus 5% ([Arcade terms §10](https://legal.arcade.ai/terms)). As guest retail, a $69 pillow
needs $100+ on the shelf, 2–3× the café norm. So the line is **owner-first**.

**Printed rug price.** No Arcade price exists for a **printed** 3×5 rug; the $576 listing is wool. The
nearest public figure is Printful's printed polyester-chenille rug, which "starts at $24.06 (excluding
VAT) for a 24″×36″ size" and reaches $75.25 at 72″×48″ ([Printful](https://www.printful.com/custom-rugs)).
That is a print-on-demand base cost, not a retail price.

## 2. What they use today

- **Print-on-demand plus a store** (Shopify + Printful/Printify, Square Online): no inventory, the owner
  designs the art ([Shopify community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-add-merch-to-my-store/599242),
  [Square seller thread](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Food-Beverage/Who-s-selling-restaurant-merch/m-p/725924)).
- **POS retail add-ons:** Toast suggests Toast Retail once a venue reaches "a few dozen SKUs" ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Expand-Your-Reach-With-Retail)).
- **Local artists** draw a mascot or motif; Merch Aid pairs artists with local businesses ([Phoenix New Times](https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/food-drink/bar-and-restaurant-merch-in-phoenix-11479795/),
  [Cool Hunting](https://coolhunting.com/style/merch-aid-apparel-for-local-businesses)).
- **Owner-drawn enamel pins** from an overseas factory ([Seattle Mag](https://seattlemag.com/food-and-culture/seattle-shops-embrace-enamel-pins/)).
- **Free palette extractors** read one image at a time ([Fundy](https://fundy.net/guides/extract-brand-palette-from-photo)).
- Agency advice: "Merch isn't just a logo anymore" ([SevenRooms Substack](https://heardbysevenrooms.substack.com/p/would-you-eat-gas-station-sushi)).
  Nobody found starts from the owner's photos.

## 3. Precedents: goods made from the room, sold to guests

- **Heath × Tartine Manufactory (2016):** the glaze palette came from the restaurant's interior; diners
  saw the plates in use first ([Heath](https://heathceramics.com/blogs/heath-journal/behind-the-scenes-tartine-glaze-palette),
  [Sunset](https://sunset.com/home/tartine-and-heaths-new-collaboration-is-drool-worthy)). The closest
  precedent: **palette of the space → ceramics → retail**, by hand.
- **The Progress (SF):** about 1,000 commissioned pieces, sold through the potter ([Remodelista](https://www.remodelista.com/posts/ceramics-from-the-progress/)).
- **La Colombe (Cape Town):** merch took off when guests asked how to buy the tableware ([50 Best](https://www.the50.com/stories/News/best-restaurant-merch.html)).
- **Ceramicists report** orders grew "because people have seen my stuff at restaurants" ([Bar & Restaurant](https://www.barandrestaurant.com/content/restaurant-ceramicists-spin-new-business-over-pandemic)).

The goods that sell are the ones guests **used in the room**. That supports owner-first.

## 4. How this group's photos exist

- **The owner's camera roll:** social posting "gets done when there is time… with phone photos"
  (vendor playbook); 23% of owners lack time for social (2014 survey) ([Restaurant Hospitality](https://www.restaurant-hospitality.com/node/10824)).
- **The curated feed:** 62% of diners sometimes check a restaurant's social first, 84% want food and
  drink photos (Toast survey, 2024) ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/zh-us/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-social-media)).
- **Listing photos** (Google Business Profile): businesses with photos get 42% more direction requests
  (Google's own all-business figure) ([SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/blog/google-continues-to-evolve-local/)).
- **Customer photos** aren't the owner's: out of scope.

An owner's everyday roll mixes the room with everything else, and a diner's roll shows many venues
briefly. So the input is a **deliberate shoot of one room**, plus a check that the reading is of one place.

## 5. Arcade category fit

| Piece | Category | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance rug (printed) | Rug | Strong: owners already buy mats; Arcade makes rugs |
| Wall print of the room's geometry | Wall Art Print | Strong for the shelf |
| Banquette pillow | Throw Pillow | Good; $69 listed |
| Plate (display, not service ware) | Ceramic Plate | **Unconfirmed.** Commercial dishwashers need durable ware ([r/Pottery](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/Pottery/comments/16f71qk/dinnerware_for_restaurants)) |
| Napkins / runner | Textile | **Unconfirmed**; volume minimums don't fit made-to-order |
| Staff pendant | Pendant Necklace | Dropped: dangling necklaces are commonly discouraged in food service |

`categories/list` decides on the day whether ceramics and table textiles exist. The line stands without
them.

## 6. Risks

- **Price:** Arcade vs café retail norms. Owner-first; mock prices shown.
- **Category existence:** ceramics and textiles unconfirmed.
- **Brand and IP:** owners will want their logo; Arcade's rules on logos are UNSOURCED, so briefs use
  motif and palette only.
- **Faces:** staff and regulars appear in owner photos; none in any piece or shared evidence.
- **Durability:** no Arcade spec covers commercial wear or dishwashers.
- **Shrinking base:** independents fell 2.3% in 2025.

## 7. Alternates

**Real-estate agents** (closing gifts from listing photos) share the most structure but overlap the
host idea. **Pet owners** is the strongest consumer alternate, not researched here.
