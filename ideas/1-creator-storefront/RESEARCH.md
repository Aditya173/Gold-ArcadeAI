# Idea 1 research: creators and personal brands → a signature capsule storefront

Public market research only. Every outside figure carries its source; weak sources (vendor blogs,
market-research summaries with no stated method) are marked "weak". Nothing here has been tested
with a buyer.

**Verdict: keep, with three conditions** (§8). The research moves the target away from "merch"
(t-shirts at about $48) and towards a **premium signature capsule** ($100–$400 pieces) for creators
whose audience already buys goods they recommend. The idea has one real weakness, which is what the
creator earns per sale at Arcade's public commission. It also has one open question: whether a
reading of a small curated feed says anything a creator couldn't type into a form.

**Plain reading.** Merch is a side income for most creators: about 1 in 5 has tried it, and about 1
in 17 says it is their top earner. The telling number is LTK's. LTK is a platform where lifestyle
creators earn a commission on products they recommend, and it shows that this group's audience
(lifestyle, fashion, home) already spends billions a year on goods a creator picked. That is the
behaviour a signature capsule sells into. A t-shirt-and-hoodie business is a different one.

## 2. Who pays, and how much

There are two payers.

**(a) The audience pays for the goods.** Arcade's docs use an $836 plain 14k gold ring as their
example, and simpler sterling pieces sit near $100. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/ai-creation-platform-arcade-expands-from-jewelry-to-home-goods)
found rugs from around $400 for a 3×9 ft hand-tufted wool rug. Typical creator merch sells for much
less:

| Comparison | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Creator merch average order | $48.50 | [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/merch-sales-creator-statistics-2026) (weak) |
| Concert merch per buying fan | $67.53; t-shirt ~$37 | [atVenu](https://www.atvenu.com/post/mid-year-venue-merchandise-insights-how-fan-spending-is-evolving) |
| Personalised pet portraits, average order | $125–150 | [Grips estimate](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/retailers/crownandpaw.com) (modelled) |
| Creator-led fine-jewelry line (QVC) | ~$300–800 | [JCK](https://jckonline.com/?p=110747) |

An Arcade capsule sits **2–8× above the usual merch ticket**, in the range of demi-fine creator
jewelry and of the home goods LTK-style creators already recommend. The quoted follower-to-buyer
rate for merch, **0.4–1.2% of followers** ([Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/merch-sales-creator-statistics-2026),
weak), is for ~$48 goods. At $100–400 the rate is unknown and probably lower.

**(b) The creator pays for the store, or doesn't.**

| Tool | Creator's cost | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fourthwall | Free, or Pro at $19/mo; print-on-demand at base cost, the creator keeps the markup; 2.9% + $0.30 card fees | [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/fourthwall-pricing/) |
| Stan Store | $29 or $99/mo | [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/stan-store-pricing/) |
| Printful (via Shopify etc.) | Hoodie base ~$26.95 + $5.49 shipping; at $45 retail ≈ $12.56 left per sale (the source's arithmetic) | [Style Factory](https://www.stylefactoryproductions.com/blog/printful-pricing), [PodVector](https://podvector.ai/articles/printful/costs-and-charges/printful-hoodie-production-cost-full-breakdown-for-pod-sellers) |
| Etsy | $0.20 per listing + 6.5% transaction + ~3% + $0.25 payment; Offsite Ads 12–15% | [Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained-2025/) |
| Pietra (creator product lines) | 2021: $49/mo for 3 products; 5% supplier commission since 2023 | [OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/creator-economy-pietra), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/06/30/pietra-raises-15m-from-founders-fund-to-help-creators-launch-their-own-product-lines) |
| A freelance designer | $100–400 per design; $50–150 per sketch | [GearLaunch](https://www.gearlaunch.com/academy/an-easy-guide-to-hiring-the-best-freelance-t-shirt-designers), [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/hire/apparel-design) |
| **Arcade** (sellers) | **Nothing up front; a 5% commission per sale** (up from 2.5%); an entrepreneur program offers "higher commission rates" to creators with "over 25,000 followers" | [TechCrunch 2025-03-24](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/ai-creation-platform-arcade-expands-from-jewelry-to-home-goods) |

**The weak point, in numbers (arithmetic on the sources above).** On a $150 pendant, 5% is
**$7.50** for the creator. A $45 Printful hoodie leaves **~$12.56**. At Arcade's public rate the
creator earns *less per sale* on a piece that costs the fan three times as much. The idea only pays
the creator well if (i) the entrepreneur program's higher rate is meaningful (undisclosed), or (ii)
a seller may add a markup on top of the maker price. Arcade's terms suggest a seller keeps any markup
plus the commission ([legal.arcade.ai/terms](https://legal.arcade.ai/terms), §10), but how that works
for a link-in-bio capsule has to be confirmed with Arcade.

**What Gold could charge** (a hypothesis). The work Gold replaces is art direction: deciding what the
capsule is and writing why. A designer charges **$600–4,000** for 6–10 designs. Creator tools cost
$19–99 a month. A one-off capsule fee of about $49–199, or a share of the commission, falls inside
both ranges. Untested.

**Who the buyer is:** the **creator with 25k–200k followers** in fashion, home, food or travel. That
tier qualifies for the entrepreneur program, has an audience that already buys recommended goods, and
earns roughly $150–1,000 per sponsored post ([Scrumball](https://www.scrumball.com/blog/how-much-instagram-influencers-make-2025),
weak). **Creators under ~10k followers are a poor fit**: at 0.4–1.2%, 5k followers give 20–60 merch
buyers even at $48.

## 3. What creators use today, and the precedents

- **Print-on-demand merch** (Fourthwall, Spring, Printful + Shopify, Printify). Fourthwall lets
  creators design products from a prompt inside ChatGPT ([Fourthwall](https://fourthwall.com/blog/chatgpt-fourthwall-integration)),
  and Printify has a free AI image generator ([Printify](https://printify.com/ai-image-generator/)).
  **Prompt-to-merch is already a commodity.** The prompt is not the edge; what goes into it might be.
- **Curated commerce** (LTK, Shopify Linkpop, Stan Store): the creator's taste sells other brands'
  products ([Shopify Linkpop](https://news.shopify.com/link-in-bio-but-make-it-shoppable-meet-linkpop-shopifys-newest-offering-for-creators)).
  Audiences pay for a creator's taste in goods.
- **Creator product lines** (Pietra; private-label jewelry; collaborations such as Mejuri's, one of
  which "went on to sell out in record time", [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/mejuri-influencer-program/)).
  These need a brand concept, design and often minimum orders. This idea makes that cheap.
- **Arcade itself** already has a creator track: early sellers include print designer Amber Vittoria
  and architect Tim Fu ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/arcade-first-ever-ai-physical-product-marketplace-raises-25m-series-a-and-expands-into-home-goods/)),
  and its founder previously built Minted ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/ai-creation-platform-arcade-expands-from-jewelry-to-home-goods)).
  **This is the main precedent and the main threat:** a creator can already prompt Arcade directly.
- **Arcade's room matcher**: upload a room photo and the design matches its colours and style. One
  photo → one product. There is no "many photos → a person → a collection".

## 4. How this group's photos exist

| Source | What it is | How we'd get it | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The posted feed** (curated) | The chosen public look, usually edited with one consistent preset (a saved set of colour edits applied to every photo) to build "a cohesive visual identity" ([Imagen](https://imagen-ai.com/post/edit-photos-like-an-influencer.md), vendor) | Instagram's API covers Creator/Business accounts only, after OAuth and app review; the personal-account API closed on 2024-12-04 ([Meta docs](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/instagram-basic-display-api), [Phyllo](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/instagram-basic-display-api-deprecation-what-it-is-for-developers-and-businesses)). For the hackathon: **a folder upload**, no app review | Strong for what the audience recognises |
| **The camera roll** | The life behind the feed; one preset seller says most content "comes from their everyday life" ([PopSci](https://popsci.com/make-lightroom-presets-influencers)). Typical rolls hold ~1,600–2,800 photos ([PhotoAid](https://photoaid.com/blog/mobile-photography-statistics/), [Photutorial](https://photutorial.com/photos-statistics); weak, figures conflict) | On-device roll access | Strong for what the creator *is*, but private: that is fork-1 |
| Shared albums, shoot folders | Outtakes, client deliverables | A folder upload | Same as the curated upload |

**Caveat from the preset finding:** a curated feed's palette is partly the preset, not the life. A
store palette taken from it is honest as "your feed's colours", never as "the colours of your days".

## 5. Which Arcade categories fit

| Creator vertical | Pieces that carry the brand | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| Fashion / style | A signature ring and earrings, plus a pendant | Rings, Earrings, Pendant Necklace |
| Food | A plate, a linen, a kitchen print | Ceramic Plate, Textile, Wall Art Print |
| Travel | A palette print and a pendant, a rug for the top tier | Wall Art Print, Pendant Necklace, Rug |
| Lifestyle / home | A pillow, a rug, a print | Throw Pillow, Rug, Wall Art Print |

A 6–10 piece capsule should mix **2–3 jewelry pieces and 4–7 print pieces**. Print pieces carry the
palette (they take `colors`); jewelry carries the geometry (it takes no `colors` or reference images).
At least two print pieces should be entry-priced so the capsule has something near the usual merch
ticket. Only Rings and Pendant Necklace appear in Arcade's docs; the other categories are named in
public coverage, and `categories/list` confirms them on the day.

**Privacy fork:** `patterns/create` accepts reference images. Sending feed photos as references
would make designs closer to them, but the photos leave the device. The default is text + palette
only; references are a per-photo opt-in on print pieces.

## 6. Risks, ranked

1. **Creator earnings at 5%** (§2): the fan pays more, the creator earns less than on a hoodie.
2. **Gold's added value is unproven against "the creator writes the prompt"** (§3).
3. **Price above the merch ticket** shrinks the buyer pool.
4. **A curated feed may measure the preset, not the person** (§4).
5. **Maker declines** ("no maker can price this"): a capsule needs spare briefs.
6. **Privacy:** the public page must carry no photos.

## 8. Conditions for keeping it

1. **Gold vs the creator's own words, blind.** Two capsules for the same person: one from the reading,
   one from a short bio plus the colours a form can collect. The creator picks "more me" without
   knowing which is which. If the bio wins as often, the reading is decoration.
2. **A reading of a curated-sized set.** The SDK has to report a reliability for the signature on a
   30–100 photo feed. Until it does, curated mode uses the palette and place types only.
3. **The commission question goes to Arcade:** the entrepreneur-program rate, and whether a seller may
   mark up.
