# Idea 5 research: short-term-rental hosts and boutique hotels -> a room-matched line

Web research only: no photos read, no models run. Every market number is a third-party figure;
where the source is a vendor or a blog, the text says so.

## Verdict: keep, with the buyer reversed

**The main buyer is the host, not the guest.**

- **Host (main):** a host or boutique hotel buys made-to-order rugs, pillows and prints for its own
  rooms. Hosts already budget for furnishing (one benchmark: about $18.4k per property). The new part
  is a **house look kept consistent across every room and property**, read from the room photos the
  host already has.
- **Guest (second):** "buy the rug from your room" is a proven pattern (Westin, Marriott Design Shop,
  Yowie, Minoan, The Host Co). It sits on top of the host's purchase as a commission line. Every
  precedent sells stock items; none sells made-to-order pieces, which is Arcade's strength and its
  risk (weeks of lead time, prices from about $100 to $1,000+).

**Weakest assumption:** that a reading of the rooms adds something a host's designer, a 30-second
colour form, or Arcade's own Magic Match (room photo in, matched rug out) cannot.

Multi-property hosts hold most listings and furnish many units to one standard: that is the "house
look across rooms" buyer. Boutique hotels are smaller, fragmented and design-led.

## 2. Who pays, and how much

**The host, for its own rooms.** About $18.4k per property (vendor benchmark); one guide advises 8 to
12% of first-year gross revenue ([rakidzich](https://www.rakidzich.com/articles/airbnb-furnishing-budget-roi-2026)).
Hosts believe design pays back through bookings; Airbnb claims professional photos can earn "up to
20% more" ([lodgify, citing Airbnb](https://www.lodgify.com/guides/airbnb/photographer/)). A rug-plus-
pillow set per unit, repeated across a portfolio, is the order shape.

**The guest, after the stay (the host earns a commission).**
- **Westin:** over $125M in retail sales 2000–2012, including 100,000+ Heavenly Beds, started because
  guests asked ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4057561.html), [Home Textiles Today](https://www.hometextilestoday.com/industry-news/nordstrom-slips-into-heavenly-exclusive/)).
- **Marriott Bonvoy Boutiques Design Shop** (May 2026): 75+ pieces "identical or similar to" room
  items; orders about 4x the site's average; sold through room-TV QR codes and invoice links
  ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/why-marriott-is-turning-hotel-rooms-into-showrooms/), [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/operate/marriott-bonvoy-boutiques-launches-design-shop-retail)).
- **Yowie** (Philadelphia, 13 suites): nearly everything is for sale; five months in it sold "a few
  pieces a week", including $550+ ceramics and $1,100 wall art ([Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/life/yowie-boutique-hotel-shannon-maldonado-20240109.html)).
  The most honest small-operator benchmark: real sales, few of them.
- **Others:** guesthouse in Baltimore, Shinola Hotel, The Manor ([Hospitality Design](https://hospitalitydesign.com/projects/shoppable-hotels-trend/157205), [Zoe Report](https://thezoereport.com/living/boutique-hotels-shoppable-interiors), [Retail Brew](https://www.retailbrew.com/issues/a-room-with-a-sku));
  Airbnb has featured shoppable homes ([Airbnb News](https://news.airbnb.com/love-the-decor-from-your-airbnb-buy-it/));
  The Host Co reports anecdotes of "an extra $500 monthly", mostly pantry items ([Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/features/retail-success-stories/the-host-co-creates-site-specific-product-sales-opportunities-at-rental-properties)).
- **Don't build on:** "67% of guests want to purchase a product they first used or saw during their
  stay" ([Rental Scale-Up, citing SpacePlace](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/shoppable-airbnb/)): vendor
  research with no published method.

**Commission today:** about 7% (The Host Co, per [Hostfully](https://www.hostfully.com/faq/what-does-it-cost));
Minoan pays hosts "a percentage". No Arcade trade or wholesale price is public.

## 4. How hosts' photos exist

- **Listing photos** (about 15 to 30 per unit, sorted by room): the best input, wide and well lit.
  **Rights:** under Airbnb's Professional Photography programme the host gives Airbnb "all rights,
  including copyright" ([Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/info/photography)); hired photographers'
  contracts vary. So the host uploads photos it owns; listing URLs are never scraped.
- **The host's own camera roll of the property:** setup and turnover shots; noisier, but it shows
  unstaged details.
- **Guests' photos** are the guests' and out of scope.

So the input is a **curated upload, per room, per property**. Magic Match uses the same input, so
photos alone are not the new part.

## 5. Which Arcade categories fit

| Category | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rug | Primary | Arcade's flagship home good. Brief it as "printed"; durability under rental traffic is unverified |
| Throw Pillow | Primary | Cheap, swappable, repeats across units, the most giftable guest buy |
| Wall Art Print | Primary | The house's signature print |
| Textile, Ceramic Plate | Secondary | Throws, table linen, breakfast ware |
| Jewelry | Out | A house look on jewelry fits neither host nor guest |

## 6. Privacy and consent

- Listing photos are the host's business photos: low sensitivity, but confirm rights at upload.
- People (cleaners, guests) are rare in listing shots; nothing is shown or sent about them, and no
  page shows any photo.
- The guest page shows pattern renders only, never guest data.
- Airbnb tightened its off-platform payment policy in May 2025, aimed at booking fees; its effect on
  product sales is unclear ([Rental Scale-Up](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/airbnb-new-off-platform-policy-may-2025/), [Airbnb Help 251](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/251)).
  Present the store as the host's own site, never an Airbnb charge.
